# Squat Depth, "Too Low," and What the Deep Sit Actually Works

**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Prompted by:** his questions after W27D2 legs — *"what is it you work on when you work on squat depth? is there such a thing as squatting too low? what are the muscles when you do a deep sitting squat / asian squat, that gets tired?"*
**Directly relevant to:** the current squat assignment (hold 80, chase depth) and the W27D2 finding that **the brace, not the ankle, is his depth limiter.**

---

## 1. What "working on depth" actually means

Depth in a loaded back squat ends wherever the **first** of these runs out:

| Limiter | What it looks like | His status |
|---|---|---|
| **Ankle dorsiflexion** | Heels lift, or the torso pitches forward to keep balance | **Not it** — *"don't really feel ankles"* |
| **Hip flexion range** (capsule, adductor length, bony socket shape) | A hard stop or a pinch at the front of the hip | Partly — he names hips |
| **Lumbopelvic control at end range** | "Butt wink" — the pelvis tucks and the low back rounds to buy extra inches | Likely |
| **The brace** | Trunk can't stay rigid; you stand up because holding the position failed, not because the legs did | **This is his** — *"the core is the limiter not legs or knees"* |

The last one is worth understanding mechanically. As you descend, the torso pitches further forward and the distance from the bar to the hips and spine gets longer. **The load on the brace goes UP the deeper you go**, even though the bar weight is constant. So a squat that's comfortable at parallel can become a bracing problem two inches lower without anything else changing.

**That means his depth work is bracing work, not mobility work.** The tools, in order of usefulness for him:

1. **Paused squats** — 2-3s sitting in the bottom at a *lighter* load. This is the single most direct way to train "hold the brace deeper." It is a form lever, not a load increase.
2. **Front-loaded squats** (goblet, or a plate held at the chest) — an anterior load forces an upright torso and a hard brace, and it self-limits: if the brace goes, you drop the weight forward and nothing bad happens.
3. **Deep-ROM leg press** — already in the program, and it has read as core/QL work rather than leg work for months. That was the tell.
4. **Dead bug / anti-extension core** — rehearses the exact ribs-down, low-back-flat position, unloaded.
5. **Unloaded deep squat holds** — teaches the hips where end range is with no load penalty (see §3).

**Not** ankle stretching, if the ankle isn't the limiter. Prep the thing that's actually stopping you.

## 2. Is there such a thing as squatting too low?

**The depth isn't the risk. Losing position at depth is.**

- **The real line: the rep where the pelvis stops staying neutral.** Under load, "too low" = past the point where the low back starts to round ("butt wink"). Loaded lumbar flexion is the mechanism people actually get hurt by — not depth itself.
- **Deep squatting is not bad for healthy knees.** The old "knees shouldn't pass the toes" rule is dead. Weightlifters squat ass-to-grass for entire careers. The deep position loads the quads and the posterior chain well. The caveat is a knee with existing pathology (meniscus, patellofemoral), where deep loaded flexion can flare things.
- **Anatomy is the one genuine "too low."** Hip socket depth and femur shape vary a lot between people. If depth ends in a **hard, pinchy, bony block at the front of the hip**, that's structure, not tightness, and grinding into it is the case where more depth is the wrong goal. Widening the stance and adding toe-out usually buys real range — that's free, try it before assuming anything.

**Rule of thumb: unloaded, essentially no such thing as too low. Loaded, "too low" is one rep past where the brace and the pelvis hold.**

## 3. The deep sit / "Asian squat" — what's actually tired

The resting deep squat is mostly a **passive** position — people who grew up with it hang on the joint and the tissue and can sit there for a very long time without much muscular effort. What makes it hard for someone who didn't is that the tissues are being asked to be at end range, not that anything is working hard.

**What's on stretch or working, roughly in order of who complains first:**

- **Adductors — especially adductor magnus.** Huge at deep hip flexion, and it doubles as a hip extensor. The most common "inner hip/groin is screaming" source.
- **Glute max and the deep external rotators** (piriformis, obturators, gemelli, quadratus femoris) — on stretch, and also quietly working to stop the knees collapsing in.
- **Hip capsule and the tissue around the socket.** That deep, diffuse, "ring around the hip" tightness is often capsular rather than muscular — which is exactly why it responds to *time in position* rather than to stretching harder.
- **Soleus and the ankle.** Heels-down deep sitting is a big, sustained dorsiflexion demand — different from what a squat rep asks, because it's held.
- **Erectors and QL**, low-grade, keeping the trunk from folding.
- **Tibialis anterior and the foot**, if you're fighting to not tip backward.

**"All the fascia around my hips gets tight and sore and I have to stand up"** is the normal, expected report for the position, and it's tissue at end range signalling — plus some blood-flow pooling from the compression. It is not damage.

**One distinction worth keeping:** diffuse, stretchy, achy tightness in the adductors/glutes/capsule is the adaptation. A **sharp, pinchy, specific point at the front of the hip crease** is a different thing — that's the impingement flavour, and the answer there is a wider stance or a slightly elevated heel, not pushing harder.

### Is it good for you? Yes, and specifically:

- It is the **cheapest hip end-range exposure that exists**, and hip end range is use-it-or-lose-it.
- It maintains ankle dorsiflexion for free.
- **It is the position the loaded squat is chasing.** The more available and relaxed the passive deep sit becomes, the more range the loaded squat has to work with. These are the same project.
- He already banked the milestone this produces — **W15D5: a pain-free deep squat hold he "could never do as an adult."** That came from the deep-ROM leg press, deep squats, cossacks, lateral lunges and hip-opener flows. The deep sit is the unloaded, free version of the same input.

### Dosing

**Frequent and short beats rare and long.** 30-60s several times a day does more for range than one grim five-minute hold — same logic as the greasing-the-groove pushups already in the program. Heels down if they'll go; sit on a book or a curb edge if they won't, and lower the support over time.

## Application to the program

- **The squat's depth work is bracing work.** W28 shifts the squat warmup emphasis to the dead bug, the internal-core rotation and the bodyweight-squat depth benchmark. The tibialis raise stays for ankle health but is no longer framed as the depth lever.
- **Paused squats are the next form lever**, not more load. 80 holds.
- **The morning flow is already most of the hip half** — cossack, lizard, pigeon, saddle, 90/90. The deep sit adds nothing new in kind, it just adds cheap volume of it.
- **Stance width and toe-out are an untested free variable.** If depth stops at a hard block rather than a stretch, widen and turn the toes out before concluding anything about mobility.
