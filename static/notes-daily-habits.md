# Daily Habits

The things that work by **frequency**, not by effort. None of this is a workout — no sets, no soreness, no warmup needed. Do a piece, walk away, do another piece later.

The rule for all of it: **short and often beats long and grim.**

---

## 🪑 Deep squat sit — the current priority

**60 seconds, a few times a day.** Sit all the way down, feet flat, and let the hips hang there.

- **Heels down if they'll go.** If they won't, sit with your heels on a book, a curb edge or a couple of plates and lower the support over the weeks. That's the ankles, and yours are the restricted piece.
- **Hold on to something** — a doorframe, a desk leg — if you'd otherwise tip backward. Hanging on doesn't make it count less.
- Wide stance, toes turned out. That's the position that gives you range.
- Diffuse achy stretch in the inner thigh and glute = right. A sharp pinch at the front of the hip crease = come out, widen the stance.

**Why it's the priority:** this is the unloaded version of the exact position the barbell squat is chasing. Every minute here is range the loaded squat gets to use.

**On doing squat reps instead:** 20 bodyweight squats an hour is a fine movement break, but reps aren't what buys depth — **time in the bottom is.** If you're going to do both, 10 reps then a 30-60s sit.

## 🦶 Knee-to-wall — the ankle test you can also train

Face a wall in a half-kneeling lunge, front toes a few inches back from it. **Drive the front knee forward to touch the wall without the heel lifting.** Slide the foot back until you find the furthest distance where the knee still touches.

- **Measure it once a month** — the distance from your big toe to the wall is the number. Compare left to right.
- **Train it daily:** 10 slow knee-to-wall rocks each side, heel glued down.

Old sprains leave lasting dorsiflexion restriction, and it's quietly the thing making cossacks hard and depth expensive.

## 💪 Pushups — greasing the groove

**One easy set, several times a day. Never to failure.** Elbows ~45°, shoulder blades pulled **down and back**, not shrugged up.

The set should end feeling like you could have done several more. That's the whole method — frequency builds the pattern, fatigue doesn't.

---

## 🪢 Dead hang

**30-45 seconds, passive**, whenever the shoulders feel compressed — after a long desk stretch, after carrying, after pull day.

Let the shoulders come up around the ears. This one is meant to be passive; the active version is a different exercise.

---

## 🧍 Desk resets

For the hours of sitting. Ninety seconds, whenever you get up:

| Move | Dose | What it undoes |
|---|---|---|
| **Half-kneeling hip flexor lunge** | 30s each side, **pelvis tucked under before you lean** | The 90° hip you've been folded into |
| **Standing cross-leg lateral lean** | 30s each side | QL and side-body compression |
| **Doorframe chest stretch** | 30s each arm, elbow at mid height | Rounded shoulders from the keyboard |

## What "crackly" means

Standing up out of a long squat and hearing a lot of crackling is **normal and benign** — mostly gas bubbles collapsing in joint fluid, the same as knuckles, which is why it fires the moment you change position after being still. It gets quieter the more the tissue moves.

Worth attention only if it comes with **pain, swelling, catching or locking.**
