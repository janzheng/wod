# Baby Interim — Home Gym Playbook

**This is a menu, not a schedule.** The numbered weeks assume you control your calendar. For the next stretch you don't. So this page replaces "what day is it" with "how much time and how wrecked am I" — and you pick from the list.

**When it takes over:** the day the regular week stops being possible. Until then, W25 runs normally. Coming back to numbered weeks is the plan, not a stretch goal.

---

## The three rules

**1. Availability beats optimality.** A 15-minute session at a weird hour is worth more than the correct session that doesn't happen. There is no such thing as a wasted short workout in this period.

**2. Nothing here is a progression.** Loads hold. The floor you built over 24 weeks doesn't fall apart in a few weeks of maintenance — it falls apart if you get hurt. This whole page is about protecting the floor, not raising it.

**3. Never train the schedule you wish you had.** Don't stack two days into one to "catch up." Don't add volume because you missed yesterday. Missed is missed.

---

## Sleep-deprivation rules

Broken sleep cuts force output and — the part that matters more — blunts the position sense that keeps form honest. You will *feel* fine and be moving worse than you think.

| On a bad-sleep day | Do |
|---|---|
| **Overhead pressing, snatches, heavy bent press** 🛑 | Cut load hard or skip. This is where the right shoulder flares, and a flare now is much worse than a lost month of pressing. |
| **Spinal-loaded work** — heavy swings, heavy hinging, and the feed bags if they land on a bad day 🛑 | Halve it. The QL has spasmed before on far less provocation. |
| **Machines, isolation, floor work** 🔥 | Fine. This is the safe half of the menu on no sleep. |
| **Flows and mobility** | Always fine. Often the best thing available. |

**The honest test:** if you can't hold the cue — shoulders packed down, ribs down, spine neutral — the load is wrong *today*. That's the same rule as always; it just bites more often now.

## What your body is actually about to accumulate

This is the part worth planning around, because it's new load, not less load.

- **Asymmetric carrying** — baby on one hip, car seat in one hand. Constant, one-sided, unplanned.
- **Long one-armed holds** — rocking, bouncing, walking a baby to sleep. Shoulders forward, one side at a time, for far longer than you'd choose.
- **Repeated bending at bad heights** — crib, changing table, car seat buckles. Short-range hinging, dozens of times a day, always slightly awkward.
- **80lb feed bags in each hand, once a week or every other.** Genuinely heavy, but his own read is *"not really a workout"* — too infrequent to count as training.

**Which means the highest-value training you can do is the stuff that undoes that:** rows, face pulls, chest openers, dead hangs, thoracic extension. That's convenient — it's all brief, all low-risk, and none of it needs fresh legs.

**Carries still need programming.** The feed bags are heavy but too irregular to count — keep a loaded carry in the menu. They're also not a fatigue source at that frequency, so don't read grip problems into them.

## The home gym

| Have | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Pull-up bar** | The single most valuable thing in there. Vertical pulling survives. |
| **Dumbbells to 100** | Heavier than you need for anything here. Bulgarians (double-50), rows, presses all fine. |
| **Kettlebells to 200** | Absurd, and useful in a narrow way. Arrillaga tops out at 50, so **60-80 is the range that actually helps** — two-hand swings, goblet squats, suitcase carries and deadlifts. **Flows stay at 25-35 regardless.** Anything near 200 is a deadlift implement, not a training bell. |

| **Cable machine** (probably) | Face pulls, if so. Confirm. |
| **Upright leg press** | More upright than Arrillaga's 45°, so shorter effective ROM. Different lift, not the same numbers. |
| **Core machine** | Unknown. |
| **Cardio** — treadmill, Woodway, rower, stair machine | Zone 2 without leaving the building. |
| **Floor mat space** | Enough for barre, flows, and Nordics. |

|---|---|
| **No rack, no barbell** | Back squat is gone. Goblet squats, Bulgarians, and heavy KB front squats cover it. |

## The menu

### 10 min — the floor
**Morning flow.** If everything else falls away, this is the one to protect. No equipment, and it targets exactly what a day of carrying and holding accumulates. Fifteen minutes on a good day, ten on a bad one, five is still worth doing.

### 15–20 min — pick one
- **One KB flow, both sides.** Bent Press Flow, Leggy Flow, B-Stance Flow, Outside Snatch Flow. Full body, no thinking, and you already know all four.
- **Barre / floor session.** Action Jacqueline-style — plié pulses, relevés, glute and core floor work. Uses the mat space, zero equipment, low neurological cost. Good on the worst-sleep days when a bell is a bad idea.
- **Postural reset.** Pull-ups or dead hangs + rows + face pulls + chest opener. This is the anti-holding-posture session — the direct antidote to a day of shoulders-forward one-armed carrying.

### 30 min — the workhorse
**KB flow + strength + complex.** This is the template that covers a full body in half an hour, and it's the one you said you actually like. Structure:

1. **Flow** (~10 min) — one of the four, both sides
2. **Strength** (~10 min) — press + row + curl, 3 rounds
3. **Complex or finisher** (~10 min) — Brutal Complex at 35, or figure-8 → tactical clean

**This is the default session.** If you only ever do this one, the interim period is a success.

### 45 min — closest to a real day
DB push / DB pull / legs, run as a normal gym day with dumbbells instead of machines. Use this when you get a genuine window.

| Day | Shape |
|---|---|
| **Push** | Incline DB press · DB shoulder press · dips or floor press · lateral raise · tricep work |
| **Pull** | Pull-ups · DB row · chest-supported row · incline curl · reverse fly · face pull if the cable exists |
| **Legs** | Goblet or KB front squat · Bulgarian (double-50) · leg press · Nordic or RDL · calf raise |

## Coverage map — the actual goal

Forget days. Over any rolling week, try to touch each of these **once**. That's the whole target.

| Pattern | Covered by |
|---|---|
| **Vertical pull** | Pull-ups, dead hangs |
| **Horizontal pull** | DB row, chest-supported row, KB row, any flow with a row |
| **Push** | Incline DB press, dips, push-ups, KB press |
| **Squat** | Goblet squat, KB front squat, Bulgarian, leg press |
| **Hinge** | KB swings, RDL, Nordic, any flow with a clean or swing |
| **Carry** | Farmer carry or suitcase carry — the feed bags are too irregular to count |
| **Core / anti-rotation** | Figure-8, windmill, bent press, dead bug, barre floor work |
| **Shoulder health** | Face pull, band work, prone Y, dead hang — the non-negotiable one |

**If a week only gets three of these, make them: vertical pull, push, shoulder health.** Those are the three that both decay fastest and fight the posture you're about to spend all day in.

## Sleep, schedule, and carving out time

Worth saying plainly: **the gym time is not selfish, and it's not optional.** Forty minutes where nobody needs anything from you is a mental health intervention that happens to also be exercise. It's also the most defensible ask you'll have — it's short, it's local, and the building gym means door-to-door is fifteen minutes plus the session.

Some things that tend to hold up:

- **Go when the baby is already being handled**, not when you're hoping for a gap. A named handoff beats a hoped-for window.
- **Short and repeatable beats long and rare.** Three twenty-minute sessions land better than one ninety-minute one, both for recovery and for the negotiation.
- **The building gym is the whole advantage.** No commute means a session survives a fifteen-minute window. Don't lose that by holding out for a "real" workout.
- **Log lightly or not at all.** A line of text is fine. Don't let the record-keeping become another obligation.

## Coming back

The numbered weeks resume when the schedule does. Nothing here needs unwinding — you'll pick up at the loads you left, and the first week back will feel harder than the numbers suggest. That's normal and it passes in about two sessions.

**Interim weeks are labelled B1, B2, …** so they're visibly outside the W-series and the numbered progression picks up where it left off.

**They get created as you go, not in advance** — and their job is to hold the *log*, not the prescription. This page is the prescription. There's nothing to pre-build: a B-week gets made when there's a session to put in it, which also means a week where nothing happens simply doesn't exist rather than sitting there as a gap.
